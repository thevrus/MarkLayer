Loom is built for one-way async video: record your screen, your webcam, or both, narrate what you're doing, and send the link. It's the default tool for a support answer, a sales follow-up, a standup when half the team is in another time zone, or any explanation where your voice and the motion of your cursor carry real information. When the point is "watch me click through this," Loom is the right shape.

The free Starter plan covers 25 videos per person with a 5-minute cap on screen recordings, for up to 50 members. Business lifts both limits at $18 per user per month, and Business + AI, which adds transcription and AI-generated titles and summaries, runs $24. For a team of ten, Business alone is $180 a month before anyone asks for the AI tier. That line item is usually what sends people looking for alternatives in the first place.

The gap worth naming is the one between two different jobs, not two competing tools. Loom captures an action over time: you must watch it, at whatever pace you recorded it, to get the information out. MarkLayer captures a state: arrows, highlights, and pinned text on a live page, readable in the time it takes to glance at a screenshot, with no play button anywhere. A three-step signup flow with a confusing transition genuinely needs the recording. A headline that says the wrong thing, or a button sitting two pixels too far left, does not, and dressing it up as a 90-second video just adds a scrubber to a fact that fits in one sentence pinned to the exact pixel.

So look back at your last ten Looms before picking a tool. If most of them were you narrating a real sequence of steps, Tella or Vidyard will record that better than Loom does and without the seat fee creeping up. If half of them were one static screen with you describing what was wrong, those were never really videos, and MarkLayer replaces them for free.
