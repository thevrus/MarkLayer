## What Feedbucket is, and the one line that matters

Feedbucket is a script you add to a site you own. From then on a feedback button lives there, and any visitor or client can capture an annotated screenshot or a video and have it routed into Jira, Linear, Asana, ClickUp, Trello, Notion, Slack or one of a dozen others. Every plan has unlimited projects and client reporters; what you pay for is team members, two on Pro and 25 on Business. There is no free plan after the 14-day trial.

That one line of JavaScript is the boundary. It is nothing on your own product and a wall on a client's production site, a competitor's checkout or a third-party tool. The [head-to-head with Feedbucket](/vs/feedbucket) goes row by row. This page is for choosing between the alternatives.

## Which one fits which job

MarkLayer is the free replacement for review that does not need the widget: open any page, annotate it, share the link. It adds live cursors and an in-room call, which Feedbucket does not advertise, and an agent on every link. It gives up screenshot and video capture, console logs and tracker sync; it files one Jira, Linear or GitHub issue per thread and nothing comes back.

Marker.io is the paid tool shaped most like Feedbucket, with developer tools attached to each report and a browser extension beside the widget. Userback is the budget route and the only one with a free plan, aimed at feedback from users rather than reviewers. BugHerd keeps the board inside the tool. If the thing you would pay Feedbucket for is video reports landing in ClickUp or Notion, Marker.io and Userback are the comparable spend. If it is console logs on a $49 plan, Feedbucket's Business at $89 is still the cheaper way to get them than Marker.io's Team.
