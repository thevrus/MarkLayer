Pastel is built for agencies and design teams that need client sign-off on creative work: a webpage, a PDF, a Figma export. Drop a URL or file into a "canvas," the client clicks and comments directly on it, and everyone works off one thread instead of an email chain three rounds deep in "see attached."

The free plan makes that workflow available to exactly one person on exactly one canvas at a time. You can archive a canvas to open a new one, and archived canvases stay readable, but only one can be active. Run two concurrent client reviews and you've already hit the ceiling free was ever going to give you.

That's usually the moment agencies start pricing out Pro, at $29 to $35 a month, or Team, which starts at $99 a month for five seats and adds $24 for every seat after that. A two-person shop reviewing one site at a time might not notice the cap for months. Anyone juggling three clients in the same week notices it immediately, and that's when the alternative-shopping starts.

The four options below cover different slices of what Pastel does. MarkLayer replaces the live-webpage annotation piece for free, with no canvas cap and no account required. Markup.io, PageProofer, and BugHerd are the paid routes, each for a different reason a team might pick it over Pastel: workspace structure, staging-site review, or a built-in bug-tracking board.
