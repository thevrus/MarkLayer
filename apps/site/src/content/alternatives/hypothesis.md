Looking for a [Hypothesis](https://web.hypothes.is) alternative? Hypothesis is already free and open source, so the real question isn't price. It's whether your annotations belong to a document or to a page.

Hypothesis is built for reading, not reviewing. Researchers use it for close reading and journal clubs; students and teachers use it to mark up assigned articles and PDFs together; open-access advocates use it because it's one of the few annotation layers built on an actual W3C standard rather than a vendor's proprietary format. Every annotation anchors to a text selection, so it survives page reformatting the way a highlighter mark on a printout never could. That's the whole design: comment on what a passage says, in public or in a private group, and let the thread sit alongside the source forever.

None of that helps if what you're reviewing is a live web page or a working product rather than a passage of prose. Hypothesis has no freehand drawing, no arrows, no way to circle a misaligned button or drop a pin on a spot in a screenshot that isn't inside a sentence. It also has nothing like real-time collaborative cursors: two people annotating the same article in Hypothesis leave separate marks, not a shared live session. That's a deliberate scope decision for a text-annotation tool, and a real gap if the job is design review, QA, or client feedback on a live site.

MarkLayer picks up exactly that gap. Paste a URL, annotate the rendered page with drawings, arrows, and threaded comments pinned to actual elements, and share a link, no account and no install required. It's the visual counterpart to what Hypothesis does for text: free and open source, but built for pages you're reviewing rather than passages you're reading.
