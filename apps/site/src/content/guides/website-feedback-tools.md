## The cheapest ones are not the free ones

Three tools here have no paid tier at all, and each pays for it somewhere.

MarkLayer is free with no accounts, seats or limits, and deletes a share link 90 days after its last access. That number is not a marketing figure. It is `RETENTION_DAYS = 90` in the open-source repository, applied by a cleanup job that runs daily, and any view or comment resets the clock.

AnnotateWeb is free, needs no signup from anyone, and states on its homepage that "annotations are automatically deleted after 2 minutes of inactivity". Good for marking up a page during a call. Useless for a review round that runs overnight.

Hypothesis is free for personal use and annotates text rather than layout. It also needs an account: "Sign up for a free account" is step one of its own getting-started page. If the thing you want to comment on is a paragraph, it fits. If it is a button that is the wrong colour, it does not.

Below those, the paid floor is lower than this category's reputation. Webvizio charges $8 a month for two users. Ybug's Basic plan is €13. BugSmash charges $14 and keeps a free tier under it. Huddlekit charges $16 billed yearly. Four tools under $20 a month, in a category most people describe as starting around $50.

## Does the reviewer need an account?

Mostly no, and that fight is over. Fifteen of the twenty-one take feedback from a reviewer who has never heard of the product, and the vendors say so in their own words. Marker.io: "Sending feedback via the widget is free and doesn't require an account." Pastel: reviewers can "comment or view without needing an account." Webvizio: "no account or installation required on their side." BugSmash: "No Sign-Ups Needed."

Two exceptions are worth knowing before you pick on price. Superflow's guests can comment, but genuinely sign-in-free guest access sits on the Scale and Enterprise tiers. PageProofer lists guest feedback as a Team and Agency feature, so the $39 Freelancer plan is not the one that gets your client commenting.

The account did not disappear, though. It moved. Read the wording carefully and it is almost always the guest side being described: BugSmash's "No Sign-Ups Needed" is spelled out on the same page as "your clients and guests don't need to signup", and BugSmash still has a login. On every tool here except MarkLayer and AnnotateWeb, the person running the review creates an account, makes a workspace, adds the project, and pays before sending the link. For a standing agency workflow that cost spreads across a year of review rounds. For a single design review on a page you do not own, it regularly costs more than the review is worth. The [no-login guide](/guides/website-feedback-without-login) breaks that asymmetry down tool by tool.

## Does the reviewer need an extension?

This is the column nobody publishes, and it decides whether a client actually leaves feedback.

Most of these tools reach the page through a JavaScript snippet you install on the site. Marker.io, Feedbucket, BugHerd, Usersnap and Userback all work this way, and once the snippet is in place the reviewer installs nothing. BugHerd's help centre puts it plainly: "All plans support the Javascript snippet - no extension required!"

So the question is not which plan you are on. It is whether you can put a script on the page at all. On your own staging site you can, and the snippet route works. On a client's live WordPress install you do not have access to, on a competitor's page, on a site behind someone else's login, you cannot, and the snippet route closes. What is left is an extension, a bookmarklet, or a proxy that renders the page for you. Webvizio is explicit about the split: public URLs load in its app for anyone, and "for password-protected environments, use the Chrome extension". Hypothesis ships an extension for Chrome and a bookmarklet for everything else.

MarkLayer sits on both sides of that line. The person creating the review runs a browser extension. Anyone opening the resulting share link gets a proxied page with no install and no account.

## What happens to your comments

The least answered question in the category, and the one this audience has been burned on.

Most of these vendors publish no retention period anywhere on a pricing or help page. The ones that do are nowhere near each other. AnnotateWeb deletes after two minutes of inactivity. Userback's free plan keeps feedback for a seven-day window. MarkLayer deletes 90 days after last access. Usersnap switches off idle free accounts after 90 days. Pastel states retention of at least one year. Markup.io and Ruttl both say they keep data "as long as we have your consent", which is a legal position rather than a number.

Two things follow. A free tier with silent deletion is worse than a cheap paid tier with a published policy, and several of the free tiers here are the first kind. And if a review round matters, export it: CSV or JSON export exists on BugHerd, Marker.io, Usersnap, Feedbucket (Business plan) and Userback (Business plan), and is missing or undocumented on most of the free options.

## What I left out, and why

A roundup that quietly swaps categories to reach a round number is padding, so seven tools that appear on other lists for this query are not in the table:

- **Loom** records async video. Useful for explaining a change, no comment pinned to an element.
- **Filestage** proofs files and video, from €199 a month. It reviews assets, not live pages.
- **Markup Hero** annotates screenshots. A screenshot stops being true the moment the page changes.
- **Highlite** highlights pages for one person, stored locally in the browser. There is no second party to share with.
- **Diigo** and **Weava** are research and highlighting tools built for students and academics. Both are good at that and neither has a client review workflow.
- **stagewise** is a browser toolbar that feeds a coding agent, with no external reviewer role in the product at all.

If the search that brought you here was "visual feedback tool" or "website design feedback tool", the twenty-one in the table are the ones that match. These seven are a different job.

## What MarkLayer is worse at

I make MarkLayer, so this section is what makes the rest of the page worth reading.

**No automatic technical context.** Marker.io, Jam, Ybug and BugHerd capture the URL, browser, operating system, screen size, console errors and network requests alongside the report. That is the biggest single reason a team pays $39 to $59 a month, and MarkLayer captures none of it. If you need a bug report a developer can reproduce, buy one of those.

**No tracker routing.** No Jira, Linear, GitHub or Trello sync, and no built-in board. Comments live in the share link. BugHerd and Marker.io both sync two ways, and Atarim ships a whole agency workflow around it.

**Ninety-day deletion.** Free and permanent are different claims, and this page should not blur them. A MarkLayer share link is deleted 90 days after anyone last opened it.

**No account means no recovery.** There is no login, so there is no dashboard listing everything you have made and no support path to recover a link you lost. The link is the whole thing.

Where MarkLayer is the right answer is narrow and real: a live page, a reviewer you do not want to make sign up for anything, no budget, and no way to install a snippet on the site. For everything else, one of the other twenty is a better fit, and the table is ordered so you can find which.
