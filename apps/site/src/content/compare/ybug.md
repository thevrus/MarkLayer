## A widget on your site versus a link to any page

Ybug starts from your own site. You paste a two-line script, add the NPM package or install the WordPress plugin, and a feedback button appears for visitors. A customer clicks it, the page is captured, they draw an arrow or black out an email address, and the report lands in Ybug with the URL, browser, OS, viewport and pixel ratio attached. Then it goes on to Jira, Linear, Slack or wherever the project sends it.

MarkLayer starts from a URL. Paste one into the web app and you are drawing on the live page, on your site or anyone else's. Share the link and the person on the other end sees the same marks and can add their own. Nobody embeds anything, and nobody signs up.

So the two answer different questions. Ybug asks "what is broken for the people using my site?" MarkLayer asks "what do we think of this page?" If you need the first, a share link won't collect it for you.

## What Ybug captures that MarkLayer doesn't

On Startup and Company, every Ybug report carries the reporter's JavaScript console and the fetch and XHR calls the page made, with status and timing. Basic records a limited console. The widget can also record the screen: the user picks a tab in the browser's own share dialog, walks through the bug with optional narration, and the video arrives in the same report as the logs.

MarkLayer has none of that. It records browser, OS and viewport width on comments left in the browser, and that is where its technical context stops. For a bug that only shows up on the third click of a checkout, Ybug's recording plus console log is the better report.

## Reporters, replies and the team-only channel

Ybug keeps two conversations apart. The team writes internal notes on a report that the client never sees. The reporter, who never made an account, gets an email when their report is received, updated or resolved, and can reply to it; the reply comes back into Ybug next to the screenshot. Both are configurable, and both are on Startup and Company.

MarkLayer has one channel. Everyone in the room sees every thread, every reply and every status change. That is right for a design review and wrong for an agency that wants to argue with itself before answering the client.

## Live review in the room

Ybug is report-shaped. Someone files, someone triages, someone fixes. Its site doesn't advertise live cursors or calls.

MarkLayer is built for being on the page together. Live cursors show who is looking where, a peer-to-peer voice or video call runs in the same tab, and the device view puts desktop, tablet and mobile side by side so a layout bug at one width is next to the other two. If your reviews are "hop on and look at this with me", that is the main reason to pick MarkLayer. If they are a queue of reports from strangers, it is no help.

## The agent on the other end

Both tools have an MCP server, but they point in different directions.

Ybug's is hosted at one address and signs in with OAuth, with no API key. It has 12 tools. Nine read: projects, members, tags, reports filtered by status or assignee, and a report's console and network logs. Those work on Basic. The other three set status, priority, type, assignee and tags, add an internal note or create a tag, and need Startup or Company. There is no delete tool, and the agent cannot file a report. The Free plan gets none of it.

MarkLayer's server is the share link itself, so `claude mcp add --transport http marklayer https://marklayer.app/s/<id>/mcp` is the whole setup. It costs nothing. The agent watches the room, acknowledges a thread so the reviewer sees it is in progress, resolves or dismisses it with a reason, and replies. It can also go first: read the page, leave its own comments anchored to elements, and propose exact text replacements the reviewer accepts as a diff. Ybug's agent works the reports your users send. MarkLayer's agent can also write some.

## Who should not switch from Ybug

Teams whose feedback comes from customers clicking a button on a production app, whose developers rely on the console log and the failed request, or whose reporters expect an email when the fix ships. That is the job Ybug's Startup plan is priced for, and €23 a month is fair for it.

Everyone else is choosing between a seat-capped reporting tool at €10 to €59 a month and a share link that costs nothing. The [alternatives hub](/alternatives) sorts the paid tools in this category by what they add.
