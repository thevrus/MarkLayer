MarkLayer and [Feedbucket](https://feedbucket.app) solve website feedback from opposite ends of who installs what. Feedbucket is a script you add once to a site you own, and from then on a feedback button lives there permanently: any visitor or client can trigger it, capture an annotated screenshot or video, and have it routed straight into one of 16-plus trackers. MarkLayer has no script and nothing to add to a site, because the page being annotated doesn't have to belong to you at all.

That one constraint, the script must be installed on the site, is the real dividing line, not price. It's a non-issue for a team reviewing its own product, where Feedbucket's standing widget and deep tracker routing genuinely earn the $49-89/month. It becomes a hard wall the moment the page in question is a client's production site you don't control, a competitor's checkout flow, or any third-party tool you need to annotate without asking someone else to add a script tag for you. MarkLayer's Chrome extension sidesteps the whole question: it overlays whatever the browser can already render, permission or not.

So the honest framing isn't "MarkLayer is the free Feedbucket," it's that they're built for opposite relationships to the site being reviewed. Own the site and want permanent visitor-facing intake: Feedbucket. Need to annotate a page you don't control, or don't want a script living there at all: MarkLayer, for free.
