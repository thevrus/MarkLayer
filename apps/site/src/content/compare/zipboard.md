## Where zipBoard charges and MarkLayer doesn't

zipBoard has no free plan. The trial runs on every plan without a card, and then Team starts at $99 a month. That buys unlimited projects and reviewers, but caps you at 20 digital assets and 1GB of storage. A web team reviewing three sites with a handful of pages each can use up 20 assets in a week.

Agency, at $199 a month, raises the cap to 50 assets and adds the integrations, project reports and manager seats. MarkLayer has no asset count, no storage meter and no plan. A share link is the whole unit, and you can make as many as you like.

## zipBoard reviews things MarkLayer can't open

zipBoard's features page lists PDFs, images, video, audio, websites, HTML files, ZIP archives and SCORM packages. The industries named on its homepage match: architecture firms, contractors, civil engineers, learning designers and translation teams. Video gets time-stamped comments. Courses open as courses.

MarkLayer reviews web pages, plus PDFs and images up to 25MB with the same pen and comment tools. That is the whole list. If your week includes a SCORM module or a 40-second product video, zipBoard covers it and MarkLayer does not.

## Approvals, versions and the paper trail

zipBoard tracks file versions, compares two of them side by side, records approvals and lets a project run through custom phases. For a firm whose client signs off on drawing revision C, that record is the product.

MarkLayer keeps a room, not a history of revisions. A reviewer can approve a thread, and an agent is never allowed to, but there is no revision C to compare against B. If the sign-off has to stand up in a contract dispute, use the tool built for that.

## What each one does with a tracker

zipBoard's Jira link runs both ways. Its help center maps assignee, priority, due date, type, replies and description, and says a change in Jira updates the zipBoard task. The same article notes that status is not mapped. Wrike, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Zapier, Procore and an API are on the list too, and the pricing page puts integrations on Agency.

MarkLayer files one Linear, GitHub or Jira issue, or one Trello card, per thread, and posts the ticket URL back once. After that the two stop talking. It posts new annotations to Slack, Teams, Discord or any webhook, free. For a team where Jira is the record, zipBoard's sync is worth paying for. For a team filing a few visual bugs a week, one way is enough.

## Live review versus asynchronous review

zipBoard is built around asynchronous review: upload, share, collect comments, assign tasks, approve. Its pages don't advertise live cursors or calls. A webpage review can record a video note with microphone and camera, which is the async version of explaining something out loud.

MarkLayer is built around the room. Live cursors show who is looking where, and a peer-to-peer voice or video call runs in the same tab. The device overview puts desktop, tablet and mobile side by side, with the open comment count at each width. And every share link is an MCP server, so a coding agent can pick up the threads, fix the code and resolve them while the reviewer watches the status change.

## Who should not switch from zipBoard

Teams reviewing courses, video, audio or construction drawings, and anyone whose process ends in a recorded approval on a numbered version. That is what the $99 to $199 buys, and MarkLayer does not try to copy it.

If your reviews are web pages and the occasional PDF, you are choosing between an asset-metered suite and a free link. The [compare hub](/compare) lists the other paid tools by what they add.
