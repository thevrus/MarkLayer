## Where BugSmash charges and MarkLayer doesn't

BugSmash's free plan is generous on storage (5GB, 100MB per file) and tight on headcount: two projects and two users. A third client project, or a third person on your side, is where the $19 a month starts. MarkLayer counts neither. A share link is the whole unit, and you can make as many as you want.

The things BugSmash puts on Starter are mostly about control: private comments, password-protected sharing, approval status, due dates and exports to CSV, PDF, Markdown or JSON. MarkLayer has no private channel, so a team that needs to talk about a client's page without the client reading along should pay for BugSmash.

## Many file types versus one live page

This is the biggest difference between the two, and it favours BugSmash. Its review canvas takes websites, video, audio, images, PDFs, slide decks, documents, emails, 3D models, CAD files and mobile apps, with version comparison on every plan. An agency that ships a site, a launch video and a brochure can collect sign-off on all three in one workspace.

MarkLayer reviews live web pages, plus PDFs and images up to 25MB. It does not review video or audio. What it adds on the web is depth: drawing tools, ruler guides, a measure tool and an inspector that writes a Markdown block for an agent, with the element's selector, computed styles and the detected React, Vue or Svelte component.

## The agent on the other end

Both tools now speak MCP to Claude Code and Cursor, and both let the agent write back. BugSmash's server covers the workspace: sign in once with OAuth, and an agent can list projects, read comments, reply, resolve, and upload a new version for the client to approve. It is on the free plan, and an admin can turn the write tools off per workspace.

MarkLayer's server is scoped to one link instead, so `claude mcp add --transport http marklayer https://marklayer.app/s/<id>/mcp` is the whole setup, with no account to sign in to. The agent watches the room live, acknowledges a thread so the reviewer sees it is in progress, resolves or dismisses it with a reason, and replies. It can also leave its own comment pins and propose exact text replacements the reviewer accepts as a diff. It can never mark a thread approved; that stays with the human.

## Live review versus async approval

BugSmash is built around a file moving from first draft to approval, with comments read and answered over hours or days. Its site does not advertise live cursors or calls.

MarkLayer is built around the room. Live cursors show who is looking where, and a peer-to-peer voice or video call runs in the same tab, so a design review happens on the page instead of over a screen-share of it. Desktop, tablet and mobile sit side by side on one screen, and a kanban board sorts every thread by status.

## Who should not switch from BugSmash

Teams whose deliverables include video, audio, slides or CAD, whose client work needs private comments and a recorded approval, or who want AI reviewers checking every upload against a brand guide. Those are what BugSmash sells, and MarkLayer does not try to copy them.

If your reviews are web pages and the paid tier would only buy you a third project, MarkLayer does that part for free.
