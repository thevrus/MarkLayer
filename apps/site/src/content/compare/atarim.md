## Two different products now

Atarim's own homepage calls it "The AI web agency, plugged into yours." Six AI specialists, Claro, Index, Lexi, Navi, Pixel and Glitch, handle project management, SEO, content, UX, design and bug fixes on client sites. Workflows run as fixed step-by-step procedures, and anything risky, like a checkout change, goes to a person first. Visual feedback is still there, but it sits inside the Pro plan next to all of that.

MarkLayer is the feedback part on its own. A share link opens a live page with drawing tools, comment pins and a kanban board, and that is the product. If you came to Atarim for the comments, most of the $67 pays for agents you may not use.

## What the credit meter means

Atarim's Free plan is one project on one site with 200 credits that do not refill, and its pricing page describes the workflows on it as "set up but dormant". Pro brings 5,000 credits a month. A credit pays for a workflow run, a site review or a "Do it" request, and Atarim prices extra credits at $10 per 1,000 for the first 5,000, falling to $6 above 10,000.

MarkLayer has no credits, plans or seats. A share link is the whole unit, and you can make as many as you want.

## Staging, WordPress and installs

Atarim reaches a page in three ways. Paste a URL, or put `atarim.ai/` in front of one, and it loads with no install; its own blog says cookie banners can interfere and dynamic pages can behave inconsistently that way. The optional Chrome extension fixes that. On WordPress, the Atarim plugin puts the toolbar on the site itself, which covers staging, and Guest Mode lets clients comment without a WordPress login.

MarkLayer's web app also fetches the page from its own server, so a password-protected page shows the password prompt there. The free browser extension handles that case on any stack: it runs in your own logged-in tab and overlays the page. On a public page, nobody installs anything.

## Trackers and agents

Atarim pushes tasks to Jira, Trello, Asana, ClickUp, Monday, Basecamp and Teamwork, and changes made in Atarim keep syncing to Jira after the first push. MarkLayer files one Linear, GitHub or Jira issue, or one Trello card, per thread, and posts the ticket URL back once.

Both have MCP. Atarim's covers the whole workspace: tasks, sites, workflows and the approval queue, behind a per-person OAuth sign-in. MarkLayer's covers one room, and the link is the setup: `claude mcp add --transport http marklayer https://marklayer.app/s/<id>/mcp`. The agent can work the comments, leave its own and propose text edits. It can never mark a thread approved.

## Who should not switch from Atarim

Agencies that want AI agents maintaining client WordPress sites under their own brand. That is what Atarim sells now, and MarkLayer does not try to copy it. Everyone else is comparing a $67-a-month agency platform with a free link, and for annotating a page the link does the job.
